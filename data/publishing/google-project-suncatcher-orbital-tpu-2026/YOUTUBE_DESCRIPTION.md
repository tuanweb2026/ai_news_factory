Could the next frontier for AI computing be orbit? 🛰️⚡

Google Research has unveiled "Project Suncatcher"—an experimental research prototype designed to test whether machine learning hardware can survive and operate in low Earth orbit.

In partnership with Planet Labs, Google has integrated four Trillium TPUs (TPU v6e) onto a prototype satellite equipped with a 1-kilowatt solar array. 

By operating in a dawn-dusk Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO), solar panels can capture up to 8× more solar energy annually than comparable terrestrial installations on Earth.

However, operating AI accelerators in space presents massive engineering challenges:
• Vacuum Thermodynamics: With no atmosphere for convective airflow or cooling fans, chip heat must transfer via copper heat pipes to external radiators that radiate thermal energy into deep space, limiting initial compute to 15-minute bursts.
• Cosmic Radiation: High-energy protons can induce single-event upsets (bit flips in memory) and permanent silicon damage.

Scheduled to launch aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 (Transporter-18), this mission is strictly a research testbed—not an operational commercial data center. If the physics hold, future orbital compute constellations linked by optical lasers could one day relieve terrestrial power grids.
