## 5. Factual Risks & Editorial Mitigations

### 1. Temporal Rule: Current Date is September 26, 2026
* **Risk:** Inadvertently phrasing the launch in the past tense (e.g., "Google launched" or "is orbiting").
* **Mitigation:** Strict forward-looking phrasing enforced: *"preparing to launch October first on SpaceX"*. Shot 6 explicitly stamps the upcoming launch date on screen: `LAUNCH OCT 1, 2026`.

### 2. Operational Reality vs Hype: Testbed vs Data Center
* **Risk:** Sensationalizing the story into "Google built a space data center" or claiming it's already serving AI traffic.
* **Mitigation:** The script explicitly refers to it as an *"AI test bed"* (Shot 1) and a *"research prototype testing if AI chips can run in orbit"* (Shot 2). Operational datacenter claims are actively refuted by `claim-19`.

### 3. "Up to 8x" Solar Metric Clarification
* **Risk:** Viewers assuming Google invented solar panels that are 800% more efficient.
* **Mitigation:** Accurately stated as annual energy capture: *"In a dawn-dusk orbit, panels can capture up to eight times more solar energy annually than on Earth."* The on-screen text reinforces: `UP TO 8× ANNUAL SOLAR HARVEST`, attributing this gain to high sunlight availability along the dawn-dusk terminator orbit without atmospheric or cloud losses.

### 4. Vacuum Thermal Dissipation Physics
* **Risk:** Hand-waving the thermal problem or implying chips can run at 100% duty cycle continuously.
* **Mitigation:** Clearly explains the absence of convective air: *"A vacuum has no air for cooling... Using heat pipes and radiators, it runs fifteen-minute bursts"*. Directly illustrates the duty-cycle limitation caused by slow radiative cooling.

### 5. Radiation Bit-Flips
* **Risk:** Glossing over semiconductor vulnerability in LEO.
* **Mitigation:** Explicitly highlights cosmic radiation bit-flips in memory (HBM) and notes that surviving and mitigating these single-event upsets (SEUs) is the core research objective.
